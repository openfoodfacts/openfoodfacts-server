# Fragrance allergens, for Open Beauty Facts.
#
# This file holds two different kinds of information.
#
# 1. Scientific evidence, from the opinion of the Scientific Committee on Consumer
#    Safety on fragrance allergens in cosmetic products (SCCS/1459/11, 26-27 June 2012):
#    https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/opinion-fragrance-allergens-cosmetic-products_en
#    The eu_lists property gives the SCCS category. The four categories are levels of
#    evidence, not regulatory classes: 82 substances established as contact allergens in
#    humans, 19 established in animals, 26 likely and 48 possible. The 48 possible ones
#    were not recommended for labelling.
#
# 2. EU labelling rules, from Annex III to Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, as amended by
#    Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1545 (OJ L 188, 27.7.2023) and its corrigendum
#    (OJ L, 2025/90876, 11.11.2025):
#      - eu_annex_iii_entry is the Annex III reference number,
#      - eu_labelling_name is the name that has to appear in the list of ingredients, when
#        that name is not one of the names of the entry. It holds the names shared by a
#        group of substances (Rose Ketones, Pinene, Terpineol) and the short forms the
#        regulation uses for a pair (Cedrus Atlantica Oil/Extract). When the label simply
#        spells the ingredient another way, that spelling is a synonym on the en: line
#        instead, so that a list of ingredients matches it.
#    A substance that has eu_annex_iii_entry must be named in the list of ingredients
#    when its concentration is above 0,001 % in leave-on products or 0,01 % in rinse-off
#    products. Products that do not comply could be placed on the EU market until
#    31 July 2026 and may still be sold until 31 July 2028, so labels made under the old
#    rules are still on the shelves.
#    Some Annex III entries cover several isomers, or several plant species. They share
#    one reference number and one labelling name, for example Rose Ketones or Pinene.
#
# Substances that are banned in the EU are kept here, because they are still found on old
# labels and in old data. They carry eu_prohibited: yes and eu_prohibited_by.
#
# CAS numbers are written as a comma separated list.
#
# wikidata holds the Wikidata item of the substance or of the essential oil. Every one was
# found through the CAS number, which is property P231 on Wikidata, so the join is exact and
# can be checked. An entry has no item when its CAS number is not on Wikidata, or when the
# only item found is too general and would fit two entries at once.
